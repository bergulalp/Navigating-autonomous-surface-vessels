Maintaining useful sensing with a surface-vehicle team requires reports to arrive before a deadline while formation changes consume time and energy. We develop a local economic controller and evaluate fungal branching/fusion, deadline-dependent link reinforcement, and ant-inspired recruitment as movement-proposal mechanisms. A conditional Bernoulli sensitivity identity maps the effect of a radio link on timely delivery into a formation direction; it is an application of established reliability mathematics. The simulator includes shared sensor visibility, correlated radio outages, one-hop-per-round caching, delayed commands and finite vessel response. Across 960 held-out one-hour missions in six scenario/hardware cells, economic control changes delivered coverage by +0.95 percentage points (95\% interval [0.77, 1.13]) relative to a static ring selected using separate development data. Relative to the earlier formation-library controller, coverage changes by +1.69 points and mean fleet energy by -62.5\%. Deadline fusion changes coverage by +0.07 points relative to hydraulic fusion ([0.02, 0.12]). The small biological gains are not robust across the diagnostic model and mission settings. The work supplies a reproducible research testbed; synthetic outcomes do not establish field performance or first use of the biological ideas in robotics.
